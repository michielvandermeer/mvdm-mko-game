# Permadeath is the only Gear sink

Gear must leave the economy somehow, or players' needs saturate, Crafting stops mattering, and
the Marketplace deflates to nothing. The obvious mechanism is durability — Gear wears out and
breaks — but degradation is a tedious tax that players experience as maintenance rather than
drama. We decided instead that Gear is destroyed only when the Explorer carrying it dies, and
that Explorer death is permanent. The sink and the stakes become the same mechanic.

## Considered Options

- **Durability and repair.** Reliable, continuously tunable, and boring. Rejected: it makes the drain feel like an upkeep chore.
- **Gear consumed per Expedition.** A strong sink, but it reads as punishing when the item was authored by the player rather than found.
- **Tier obsolescence alone.** Not a sink at all — obsolete Gear still exists, and Salvage recycles it. Insufficient on its own.

## Consequences

The drain rate is now something *players choose*, which is the uncomfortable part. A player who
farms a safe Area indefinitely never loses Gear, never needs to craft again, and contributes
nothing to the Marketplace. The design therefore carries a standing obligation: rewards must
scale faster than risk, aggressively enough that pushing into lethal territory is compulsory
rather than optional. Safe Areas being *survivable but unprofitable* is not a balance nicety
here — it is what keeps the entire economy running. Expect this to need continuous tuning, and
expect the economy curve test to be the place it gets watched.

Permadeath also means loss-aversion is a live risk: players who feel they cannot afford to lose
an Explorer will stop taking risks, which starves the sink. Recruitment must stay cheap and
reliable enough that a death is a setback rather than a ruined save.
